the normal workflow: two clicks per turn

turns tab — press i where a turn starts and o where it ends, then press measure. The tool runs the frozen estimator on exactly that window and tells you which way the camera turned, with a confidence. Press accept if it matches what you saw, or override it by hand if it does not. That is the whole job, and it does not scale with the length of the clip.

You do not have to decide the direction yourself. The estimator has been checked against hand labels on readable windows and agreed on 7 of 8, so it is a good first opinion; your job is to catch the cases where it is wrong, and those get recorded as an override so the disagreement stays visible.

why the trajectory tab is not the main tool

Drawing the path by hand is richer in principle: the shape gives direction, turn rate and total angle at once. But it needs roughly one click per second of video, which is about 1500 clicks for this clip, and it cannot represent standing still — the points pile up in one spot. It is kept for short dense stretches where you want the rate, not for marking the whole route.

direction

Everywhere in this tool a label means the direction the camera turned. For a yaw the image moves the opposite way: if content slides toward the left edge of the frame, the camera turned right. The measure button already accounts for this and reports the camera direction directly.

which turns are worth marking

Clean ones: one sustained sideways slide in a single direction, no strong up or down pitch, no walking bob dominating. Two clean turns of each direction are worth more than ten muddy ones. If a stretch is muddy, mark it unclear rather than guessing — that is a useful answer. Watch out especially for windows that are mostly pitch: a camera tipping down shows vertical streaks, not a sideways slide, and will not give a usable yaw.
